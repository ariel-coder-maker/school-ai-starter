Store your school once. Every request after that is one line.

A chatbot only knows what you type. This kit stores your calendar, criteria, standards, resources and house style in a folder your AI reads first, so a teacher can ask for "lesson 3 of my Grade 7 forces unit" and get something that fits how your school teaches. It works for IB MYP out of the box, and for any other program with your own framework.

Building your own from scratchWith or without the kit · any AI coding tool

You don't need the kit to build this. Your setup sheet is your specification: they hold everything the AI needs to know about your school. What follows is the design that made the kit work, as six rules and one prompt.

Six design rules, learned the hard way

1Store it once, in files. Calendar, criteria, resources and style live in a folder the AI reads before every request. Requests stay one line long. 2Make it in steps. Outline, then lessons, then assessment, approved one at a time. Rejecting an outline costs a minute; rejecting a finished unit costs the unit.
3Every mistake becomes a check. Write each rule as one testable sentence. The AI runs every check before you see the work. 4Never let it invent what must be real. Criteria, standard codes, textbook content and equipment come from your files, or they don't appear.
5The cheapest model that passes the checks. The checks, not the price of the model, keep quality up. Swap models as they improve. 6A person approves; no student data goes in. The AI prepares, a teacher decides. No names, grades or reports in the folder.

The prompt that starts it

Make an empty folder, open it in Claude Code, Codex, Cursor or similar, and give it this with a photo of your setup sheet:

Build a school curriculum assistant in this folder. Create START_HERE.md with the rules, and folders 00_school, 01_curriculum, 02_sources, 03_resources, 04_prompts, 05_checks and 06_outputs. Use my setup sheet (photo attached) to fill 00_school and 01_curriculum, and ask me one question at a time about anything missing. Then write prompts that make a unit in steps (outline, lessons, assessment), stopping for my approval at each step, and a checks file every output must pass before I see it. Never invent criteria, standards or equipment: if it isn't in my files, ask.
